
PocketHoller

Full game agent handoff

Working subtitle  After Sundown
Prepared for Marty and Roamin  •  Design version 1.0  •  9 October 2026

Build a complete, small Game Boy Color adventure about a child, his dad, and the strange but dependable creatures of a mountain town. The woods repeat familiar voices after dark. Roamin restores three warning lights, learns how the imitation works, and brings the whole town through one uneasy night.

The production decision

Use a native editable GB Studio project through the ModRetro Chromatic plugin. Finish and review a ten-minute playable opening before building the rest. The finished game should take a new player about 60–90 minutes, have a real ending, and ship with editable source and a real ROM once the owner approves release.

How the game plays

PocketHoller is a top-down creature-puzzle adventure. Movement and interaction are simple; the challenge is noticing what changed, choosing a trusted companion, and carrying a learned rule into a new room.

The repeating loop

Explore a small connected area → notice a blocked route or contradiction → inspect its clue → choose a companion at a safe camp → use its ability on a marked target → open a permanent route → return to safety and save. Each area teaches one use, combines it with an earlier rule, then pays it off in a warning-light repair.

A new button press triggers one action; holding B never repeatedly restarts an ability. Consume held inputs across dialogue and scene transitions so a closing A press cannot instantly operate the next object. Show an icon plus a short verb when a companion target is in range.

Companions and inventory

Only one companion follows at a time. Swap freely at the porch or a lit camp. All recruited companions remain available, healthy and friendly. Abilities have no charges, stamina, cooldown puzzle, or collectible fuel. Story objects are flags in the journal, never droppable inventory items. There is no money or equipment menu.

Goals and help

The journal shows one specific next objective, the three light states, companion descriptions, and six optional pages. Inspecting a blocked route states what is missing without spoiling the answer. Each camp offers a two-step hint: a nudge first, then an explicit next action on request. No hint changes rewards or the ending.

Pacing and navigation

Use mostly single-screen rooms at 160 × 144 pixels. Up to four rooms may scroll to at most two screen widths. Target one meaningful observation or interaction every 20–40 seconds. Previously solved routes stay solved; later visits use shortcuts. Return to Camp is always available outside a brief transition and preserves completed puzzle flags in the current session.

Story and characters

Beginning

It is the evening of PocketHoller’s lantern walk. Marty is checking the porch signal while Roamin explores the orchard. A storm has tangled the old warning-light circuit. In a disused lantern, Roamin meets LanternMoth and learns to reveal marks hidden beneath the ordinary world. When the first light is repaired, sunset settles over the valley. From beyond the fence, a voice repeats Marty’s invitation to come home, one word too many.

Middle

The town gathers at the festival green while the woods begin returning people’s own phrases to them. Marty keeps the home signal lit and gives Roamin safe routes and practical help. Roamin meets MossbackTurtle beside the creek, restores the lowland light, then reaches BabyThunderbird at a silent ridge roost. The ridge light reveals that the old hollow bell has been disconnected from its spring. The forest is treating every departing footstep as a person it must keep.

The truth and ending

The antagonist is the Hollow Caller, an original sound-shaped presence that grew around an abandoned bell and the voices it caught. It can copy words, rearrange signs and make a wrong doorway look welcoming. It cannot invent a companion’s living glow or turn a creature against Roamin. Its idea of safety is keeping every voice in the woods forever.

At the hollow, LanternMoth reveals the true bell inscription; MossbackTurtle restores water to the bell’s spring; BabyThunderbird charges its three lamps. Roamin chooses to ring the bell for everyone, rather than follow a borrowed voice. The town answers with its own signal. The Caller breaks into harmless echoes, leaving only wind in the hollow. Dawn returns. Roamin walks home, reunites with Marty, and joins the creatures for breakfast. No sacrifice, secret bad ending, or unresolved rescue is required.

The dependency chain

P01 Moth → P02 Light 1 → P03 Turtle → P04 causeway → P05 wheelhouse → P06 Light 2 → P07 Thunderbird → P08 Light 3 → P09 true inscription → P10 spring → P11 bell charge → P12 bell and dawn.

P04–P06 require Turtle selected. P07 is solved with ordinary interaction. P08 and P11 require Thunderbird. P09 requires Moth; P10 requires Turtle. R19 is a permanent camp connecting all three finale spokes, so each companion change is short and safe.

State rules

Use separate flags for recruited companions, completed puzzles and repaired lights. A room derives its layout and dialogue from those flags on every entry.

Puzzles are idempotent: a second interaction acknowledges success, without removing items, replaying a reward, or toggling a completed route shut.

The story phase starts DAY. P02 sets DUSK, P06 sets NIGHT and P12 sets DAWN. No real-time clock, calendar date or wall-clock dependency exists.

Every chapter ends at a safe save opportunity. The transition tells the player whether progress has been saved. Optional journal pages never gate a chapter or change the ending.

The companion system

Ship exactly three companions in version 1.0. They are partners with distinctive useful behaviors, not combat units or a collection treadmill. The full cast stays below the six-creature ceiling; additional creatures are out of scope for this build.

Interaction contract

Face a target within one gameplay tile and press B. If the active companion matches its required ability, lock input briefly, play a readable response, set the puzzle state, update the visual and release input. If it does not match, show one short contextual line, then return control. An empty target produces a small companion emote with no blocking dialogue.

Use three target shapes as redundant cues: rays for Reveal, a wave line for Steady, and a zigzag for Spark. Match them in the journal and camp menu. Color is additional information only. A companion never acts off-screen to solve an unseen puzzle.

Follower implementation

Start with a simple non-colliding follower that trails one tile behind and snaps safely to Roamin after a transition. If smooth following threatens schedule or sprite budgets, use a nearby hovering/perched presentation in the same room; keep the companion visible at ability use. It never blocks a corridor, triggers a door, consumes a pressure plate, or becomes a pathfinding dependency.

Camp contract

R01, R07, R13 and R19 are always safe. The camp menu offers Companion, Hint, Save and Leave. Only recruited companions appear. Swapping does not advance the chapter or reset local progress. Before entering a gated area, the nearby camp and journal make the required companion clear. After every recruitment, activate that companion and show its practice target.

Trust is a mechanical rule

Companion glow, emotes, silhouettes and target cues are never falsified by the Caller. A creature’s refusal can warn of a false door, but its guidance is always correct. Their idle animations can be funny or sleepy. They are never injured, possessed, traded away, or used as the cost of an ending.

Backtracking and recovery

Returning along an opened edge never requires the currently active companion. A companion check gates the original solve action, not the collision of a route already opened. The final hub keeps all companion changes within one room of each spoke. The first legitimate arrival at R19 sets VISITED_R19 and opens both ends of the marked R19–R01 shortcut. This preserves the first R17/R18 story visit. The shortcut stays open after the ending.

Return to Camp sends Roamin to the most recent visited safe camp. Retain recruited companions, completed puzzles, journal pages and lights in memory; discard only unfinished local switch arrangements and transient encounters. This action does not imply a disk save. Offer Save at the camp on arrival.

Optional content is bounded

Six journal pages: J1 town lantern tradition at R03; J2 fictional porch note at R24; J3 old trail sketch at R14; J4 bell rhyme at R17; J5 Caller origin at R18; J6 dawn drawing at R23. Each adds two or three short text panels. None supplies the only copy of a mandatory clue; all can be collected in the dawn state.

Horror direction and encounter rules

The horror is the familiar becoming slightly wrong. Safe places are genuinely safe. A scare should invite an observation, then give control back. Design for a family to choose its comfort level; do not assume a particular age rating before review.

Trigger discipline

Each encounter has a one-shot flag. Set it before the presentation begins, so leaving or pausing cannot repeatedly retrigger the scare. Run only when no dialogue, menu, ability or transition owns input. Never stack encounters in the same room entry. Target at least 60 seconds of ordinary play between forced atmospheric beats; the four authored locations provide the main separation.

A short beat may lock movement for at most two seconds, but Start must still pause. No flashing, rapid full-screen palette inversion, sudden maximum-volume burst or controller combination. Before the final sequence, show a clear confirmation so the player can save or change settings.

Failure without punishment

There is no health or death state. A false doorway returns Roamin to the local room entrance or R19, accompanied by a calm creature cue. A wrong switch sequence resets only its unfinished local arrangement. No completed puzzle, recruited creature, journal entry or save is removed. The Caller does not chase the player, so no speed, dexterity or reaction-time gate blocks completion.

Trust and tone checks

No imitation of Marty uses Marty’s portrait or his safe-hub speaker label. Real Marty stays helpful and visibly located at the porch.

No fake crash, fake deletion, save tampering, pretend real-world surveillance, or use of the player’s actual date, name or files.

Never punish pausing, using hints, muting audio, choosing Reduced Scares, or returning to camp.

After tension, include a warm creature idle, a lit shelter or a reassuring objective. Dawn clears all remaining scare triggers.

Art direction and production assets

Use autumn amber, creek teal, moss green and blue-black shadows. Day feels handmade and welcoming. Night changes the palette and a few meaningful details, while paths, faces and interactable silhouettes stay readable. Horror should come from composition and timing rather than visual noise.

Character art

Roamin is the smaller explorer silhouette, with curly brown hair, a sage jacket and compact pack. Marty has a broader, grounded silhouette, short swept hair, rectangular glasses, close facial hair and a rust field jacket. These cues follow the photo-referenced concept sheet; keep the final sprite stylized. Develop stylized likeness from the supplied reference photo only with owner review. Do not infer age, ethnicity, health, or personal history from the image. Final 16 × 16 gameplay frames prioritize posture, hair/hat outline and clothing blocks over miniature portrait detail.

Create six-frame 96 × 16 directional walk sheets for the player and any walking NPC, plus 16 × 16 idle or emote frames as needed. Simple directional sheets store down, up and right views; left may mirror right. Preserve a non-mirrored accessory if the approved design requires it through native metadata. Import and hardware rules

Screen resolution is 160 × 144; background tiles are 8 × 8. In automatic color backgrounds, each tile uses at most four colors and a scene has at most eight four-color palette families. Use the native manual source palette when choosing manual coloring: #071821, #306850, #86c06c, #e0f8cf. Sprite imports use #071821, #86c06c, #e0f8cf and the transparent marker #65ff00; #306850 is not a sprite source color. Confirm the selected project’s actual import convention. [S1, S2]

A hardware object is 8 × 8 or 8 × 16; a 16 × 16 actor commonly spans two objects. Hardware allows 40 objects total and only 10 on one scanline. Count actual metasprite pieces rather than actor names. Inspect genuine moving frames for flicker. [S4]

Settings available before New Game

Scares: Standard or Reduced. Reduced removes abrupt stings, extra-step timing and sudden motion; every clue, chapter and ending remains available.

Audio: Music On/Off and Effects On/Off. Quiet preset turns both off. No puzzle depends on sound alone.

Text: Normal, Fast or Instant reveal. A completes the current line before advancing it. Never require rapid presses.

Controls: Standard or Swap A and B for action/confirm and ability/back. Show matching labels throughout; retain Start for pause and Select for objective.

Visuals: No flashing or screen shake in any mode. Keep UI high contrast; the scene’s eighth background palette is reserved for UI/fonts in color mode. [S6]

Text production rules

Budget roughly 1,800–2,600 words for the whole game, including optional pages and hints. Prefer one idea per panel and two or three short panels per conversation. Use an original or properly licensed readable pixel font; count actual rendered glyph widths. Validate long names, settings labels, every clue, save notices and credits at the real 160 × 144 resolution. Do not rely on desktop-size mockups.

Family review

The owner reviews the first ten minutes with Standard and Reduced Scares before art or narrative expansion. Ask whether tension feels fun, whether companion trust is clear, and whether Roamin’s role feels right. Use this review to adjust presentation, not to add an unbounded second game. An adult should review content before deciding who it is suitable for; no formal age-rating claim is made here.

Use named native variables and freeze their IDs for released saves. This is a logical schema, not a custom serializer. Reinitialize temporary switches, input locks, follower position and menus safely on entry/load; they are not authoritative progression.

Loader and compatibility

GB Studio provides slot existence checks, variable reads from a save, and an On Load branch. Use reserved schema and epoch fields to reject known-incompatible saves before loading; use the On Load branch to restore a safe camp state and validate invariants. A schema match alone does not prove engine snapshot compatibility. Verify updates against archived release saves. For an incompatible save, explain the issue and offer New Game without deleting the old file automatically. [S7]

Never claim untested durability

Do not claim atomic or corruption-proof saving. Test empty slots, save/load, reset after completed saves, and interrupted writes on disposable copies where supported. Verify persistent storage on the selected cartridge separately from browser and emulator saves. [S7]

Required invariants

P02 implies Moth; P06 implies Turtle and P04–P05; P08 implies Thunderbird; P12 implies P09–P11 and all lights. No gate can close behind a player without an unconditional return edge. Camps remain reachable with every recruited companion. Return to Camp clears only unfinished local state. After P12 and until ENDING_SEEN, the journal and Return to Camp retain the Go home to Marty objective; leaving R23 cannot skip the reunion. After ENDING_SEEN, Continue uses R01 regardless of CAMP_ID. No erase or New Game overwrite happens without an explicit player confirmation.

Emulator workflow

Build with rom_build and inspect that exact cartridge with rom_inspect. Use the plugin’s emulator_run, emulator_step and emulator_observe for input-driven checks. emulator_observe is inert; step calls advance the game and specify the complete held-button set. After a failed image delivery, observe rather than blindly repeating an input whose execution is unknown. Rebuild after fixes and repeat comparable input sequences on the new hash.

Preview and hardware boundaries

Use web_preview early for human review and open its URL only in the supported built-in browser. If that browser is blocked or unavailable, retain the limitation and continue authorized headless PyBoy checks on the actual ROM. Do not claim that this proves browser UI behavior, audio listening, cartridge timing, or physical Chromatic compatibility. Physical deployment or flashing is a separate step requiring an available device and appropriate authorization.

Release blocking failures

Any crash, inaccessible mandatory clue, unrecoverable gate, save-loss regression, unreadable key text, missing source asset, unlicensed content or mislabeled test evidence blocks release. Cosmetic issues may remain only in an explicit known-issues list that does not excuse a broken core path. A missing physical test means “emulator tested; physical validation pending,” never “Chromatic verified.”

Proposed licensing split

Propose MIT for original project code and scripts, and CC BY 4.0 for original cleared art, music and narrative assets. The owner must approve this split before publication. MIT requires preserving its notices. CC BY 4.0 allows sharing and adaptation, including commercially, with attribution, a license link and indication of changes; compliant reuse cannot simply be revoked. Creative Commons does not grant unrelated privacy, publicity or third-party rights. [S8, S9]

Do not put a blanket license over files the project does not own. Preserve every dependency’s original notices and terms, including the engine/runtime, starter/template material, plugins, fonts and sound libraries. Record an explicit license per file or asset group and distinguish tool licenses from material shipped in the ROM. Remove or replace anything whose origin or redistribution rights are uncertain.

Provenance gate

Maintain an asset inventory with path, title, creator, creation date, source URL or generation method, original license, modifications, attribution text, permission evidence and release clearance. Record AI-assisted concepts and human cleanup honestly. Do not assume generated work has protectable copyright or promise exclusivity. Use factual credits and license only rights actually held.

Original means original

No ripped Pokémon sprites, sounds, creature designs, names, maps, fonts or recognizable derivative branding. Do not ship proprietary Nintendo boot ROMs or other commercial game ROMs. PocketHoller and the proposed subtitle need a reasonable title/brand conflict check before release. Family likeness approval is separate from copyright licensing; use fictionalized alternatives if public use is not approved. Keep permission records private and put only necessary attribution in the public repository.
